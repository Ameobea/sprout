1 · Where the training loss actually comes from

The presence head's multinomial loss has a hard floor: with n profile items as targets, softmax mass must spread over all of them, so per-item NLL ≥ log n even for a perfect model. Averaged the way the loss weights users, that floor is E[log nu] = 5.05 nats — 83% of the converged training loss. Corruption — the thing the DAE is named for — adds just 0.17 nats. The remaining 0.87 nats is reconstruction imperfection through the 512-d bottleneck: it persists with a fully clean input.

2 · What the model knows about unseen items

On dropped items at training corruption, popularity alone scores 7.78 nats; the model reaches 7.03 — it extracts ≈1.1 bits per unseen item from the visible profile. That information is not uniform: in the deep tail it extracts 2.7 bits/item, six times the head tier. Personalization signal lives almost entirely in the tail — quantitative support for the hidden-gems objective and the logQ prior. (The no-prior replica shows the same tier profile within 0.04 bits: the prior redirects serving, it doesn't change information content.)

3 · The saturation is a training artifact, not a data limit

The model's context curve flatlines: revealing more than half a profile adds almost nothing to its unseen-item NLL or ranking. It would be tempting to call that the data's conditional entropy. It isn't. EASE — a closed-form, full-rank linear autoencoder (diag-zero constraint, so structurally trained at keep ≈ 1) — keeps improving all the way to full context and overtakes the NN's ranking there decisively. The mechanism: with dropout 0.4 ± 40%, the NN never sees more than 76% of a profile during training — though a follow-up probe (§5) shows fixing that alone does not close the gap.

At the serve regime (full profiles) the linear model ranks better: median rank 33 vs 59, recall@50 0.551 vs 0.470. The full-context headroom is real — a linear model reaches it. The obvious explanation (the NN just isn't trained at high keep) was tested directly and refuted: a probe trained across keep 0.52–0.975 ranks identically at full context (§5). What EASE exploits appears to be fine item-item detail that the 512-d bottleneck cannot carry — an architectural gap, not a curriculum one. NLL stays in the NN's favor everywhere — EASE's two-parameter global calibration can't match per-user mass allocation, so ranks are the fair cross-model comparison.

4 · The rating noise floor — how good could predictions ever get?

Two users whose profiles look identical to the model can still rate the same show differently; no model can beat that spread. Near-twin profile pairs measure it. One catch the naive analysis misses: above Jaccard ≈ 0.6, "twins" are mostly the same human twice — copied and migrated lists with near-zero disagreement. Filtering to genuine pairs, held-out-item disagreement tracks the pair's rating agreement on other items almost perfectly, and item-set overlap adds nearly nothing: the ratings channel is the sufficient statistic. For the most agreeing genuine pairs, disagreement bottoms out at σε ≈ 0.34–0.38 → an aleatoric floor of ≈ 0.25–0.30 z-MAE — and that's for the most predictable pair population, so the population-wide floor sits higher.

6 · Implications — where to direct effort

  • Stop worrying about the corruption. It costs 2.8% of the presence loss. The denoising objective is a training device, not a meaningful loss inflation. (For ratings it's ~half the loss — but that half is the prediction task.)
  • The full-context ranking gap is real but architectural. A plain linear model beats the NN at serve-regime ranking (median rank 33 vs 59) — and the cheap fix is already ruled out: a dropout-mixture probe trained at high keep ranks identically (60 vs 60). Capturing the gap means either blending/distilling an EASE-style item-item head at serve time (no retraining; the two models' strengths are complementary by context level) or architectural work (wider bottleneck / skip connections), judged on rank-at-full-context — a metric the earlier "capacity moved nothing" test never measured.
  • Rating headroom is real but bounded. Floor ≈ 0.25–0.30, model at 0.425, and a trivial additive baseline at 0.502. Most remaining error is either aleatoric or needs information CF doesn't carry (content features, review text) rather than more capacity on the same signal.
  • The tail is where the model earns its keep — 2.7 bits/item vs 0.45 in the head. Anything that improves tail coverage (content features for thin-evidence items) leverages the model where it's already differentiated.
  • The Kendall log-vars are a balancer, not a noise meter. They converge to exactly the per-task losses (es = L, each weighted term = 1.0). The twin floor is the number that actually bounds achievable rating error.
  • Data quality note: near-duplicate profile pairs above J ≈ 0.6 are overwhelmingly list copies. They're ~a few hundred exact groups plus a long near-dup tail — not a training hazard at current scale, but any future twin/similarity analysis must filter them.

9 · Blending: better than both — with one tail caveat

Per-user z-normalized score mixing, s = (1−w)·z(NN) + w·z(EASE). At full context the blend beats both endpoints and the optimum is broad (w ≈ 0.2–0.65 all land at median rank 29–31). The mid-band (popularity rank 250–1k) is where blending shines: recall@250 0.903 vs NN 0.802 / EASE 0.864. The caveat is the deep tail, where EASE's ridge shrinkage drags the blend down (0.234 vs the NN's 0.360) — a serving design that blends less (or not at all) in the tail region, or per-tier weighting, would likely keep both wins. At small k, blending still wins aggregate rank but sacrifices most of the NN's tail reach — so blend weight should scale with profile size, reaching ~0 for cold-start users.

Round 4 · Value metrics, the bottleneck reality check, and post-bottleneck grafts · 2026-08-03

14 · What recall can't see, what width can't fix, and grafting after the bottleneck

Three questions this round: (1) is raw recall the right headline metric, given that popularity regurgitation scores well on it; (2) does bottleneck width actually bind — testing the round-2 "architectural bottleneck" claim against the older intuition that size didn't matter; (3) can EASE be grafted into the model in a way the (failed) input-channel graft couldn't — injecting it after the bottleneck, where compression can't crush it. New instrument: probe_value_eval.py, a unified battery reporting franchise-filtered popularity-tier recall, novelty-stratified recall (target's gram-cosine distance to the nearest kept item — "did we find things far from what the user already has", the adjacent-cluster discovery case), and filtered top-10 nicheness, at five context sizes on 3,000 held-out users.

Both prior beliefs were half right. Below 512 the bottleneck genuinely binds (128 costs ~0.24 nats and 21 median-rank points). Above 512, total loss keeps falling — but every nat of it is kept-item reconstruction, i.e. input memorization; dropped-item NLL and ranking don't move (differences ≈ seed noise; the two 512 seeds differ by 0.002 nats / 4 medrank). This reconciles the old "size didn't matter" intuition (true, for ranking, in the upward direction) with round 2's bottleneck framing — which now needs a correction: the NN's full-context gap to EASE is not a bottleneck-width problem. Even at width 2048 (= hidden dim) the medrank gap to EASE persists. The item-item detail is lost in the sum-pooling encoder — superposition of item embeddings — not squeezed out at the latent. Widening the pipe doesn't help because the information never reaches it.

Metric proposal — what "good" should mean for this product. Headline: franchise-filtered recall@250 in the 250–6k popularity band at matched filtered overall recall (the filter kills the next-season confound that inflated every EASE number; matching kills the popularity-remix confound the (α,k) work exposed; the band excludes the head tier, where recall is ~free). Secondary diagnostics: novelty-q4 recall (adjacent-cluster discovery — the "edges of communities" value case), filtered mean top-10 popularity rank + top-10 novelty (list character), and unfiltered k8/k16 recall as the interactive-recommender guard. Raw overall recall stays as a guardrail only — at k8 it rates a top-10 with mean popularity rank 13 as "fine"; the value metrics rate it as zero.

Methods

Vectors: user_input_vectors_cleanup_notrust.npz (1.574M users, 366.7M entries, median profile 164 items). Decomposition: analysis/loss_decomposition.py, 20k users (seed 123), exact train-style corruption (rate 0.4 ± 40%, per-channel), latent noise on/off, clean pass; per-user weighting identical to the training loss; reproduces logged losses (6.093 vs ~6.10; 0.0794 vs ~0.079). Floors/baselines: analysis/info_floors.py, analysis/rating_ladder.py (item means shrunk λ=50 on a 95% user split; additive adds shrunk leave-one-out user offsets, and the protocol-matched variant computes offsets from kept items only). Twins: analysis/twin_noise.py — MinHash LSH (48 hashes, 12×4 bands) over presence sets → 17.1M candidate pairs + 284 exact-dup groups; disagreement on shared rated items, leave-one-out context conditioning; σε = √(Var(Δ)/2), Gaussian MAE conversion σ·√(2/π). EASE: analysis/ease_baseline.py — Gram on 1.55M train users (eval/calib users excluded), λ ∈ {200…3000} (flat), softmax(τ·s + β·log pop) calibrated on held-out users; identical corruption protocol and candidate masking as the NN rank sweep. Scaling: analysis/train_probe.py — production recipe, fixed 10% user holdout (seed 999), 2k-user fixed eval batches every 2k steps. NN rank sweep on 5k users (CPU JAX). Rank metrics use per-dropped-item rank among non-visible candidates — internal protocol, not comparable to the LOO eval harness's recall@50. Round 2: analysis/blend_smallk_eval.py (5k users/config, seed 123 pool; NN score = logits + log pop, EASE = s + 0.217·log pop — the ranking-equivalent of the τ=3.0/β=0.65 calibration; blends on per-user z-normed scores; absolute-k contexts uniform-sampled from profiles with ≥ k+8 items), analysis/ease_recs_eyeball.py (unfiltered top-15s), analysis/train_probe_ease.py (3-channel graft: ch3 = per-row z-normed xpresence·B computed in-graph from the corrupted presence; same holdout protocol), analysis/corpus12k_ease.py (streamed raw dump; presence = entries except unrated PTW; ≤2000-entry and ≥20-in-corpus gates; 12k gram excludes the 20k eval users; 6k-pool ranks mask non-6k candidates for comparability).
