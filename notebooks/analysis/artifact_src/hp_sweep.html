The muon story

Rating error vs training step4× step efficiency

held-out rating MAE (fixed 2048-user eval) across training · lower is better

Muon (Newton–Schulz orthogonalized momentum) is the only thing tried that beats the adam ceiling — on both heads at once, twice. Two independent seeds landed within 0.0002 of each other (0.4459 / 0.4457 trusted MAE, ρ 0.6098 / 0.6099, presence NLL 7.0545 both) at just 60% of the control's step budget. Nothing else in two nights of experiments moved both heads together. The catch: NS runs five large dense GEMMs per step on the 12000×2048 and 2048×6000 weight matrices — ~10× wall-clock on this GPU and enough sustained occupancy to crash the desktop environment sharing it (a first).

Verdicts

  • Muon: tentative policy — not the everyday recipe, but likely worth paying once per model cycle for the final production retrain (overnight, machine idle): a permanent, serve-free quality gain roughly the size of an entire experiment span's winnings. DECIDER PENDING The batch-1024 muon run decides: NS cost is per-step, so doubling the batch should roughly halve wall-clock at equal sample count. If quality holds at ~3–5× adam's wall-clock, muon becomes the final-retrain recipe; if not, it's dropped without regret.
  • Cosine schedule beats reduce-on-plateau — confirmed on two seeds (0.4499/0.4497 vs 0.4507/0.4516), presence equal-or-better, LR-insensitive between 3e-4 and 4.5e-4, and free. Adopt for all future training regardless of the muon decision.
  • The current adam recipe was already at its optimum. 2× LR hurts under plateau; 3.3× diverges outright under cosine. Drop-in optimizer swaps at sensible LRs — ademamix, lion, schedule-free adamw — all lose. Gelu ≈ swish. The recipe wasn't leaving easy performance on the table; it took a different optimization geometry to move.
  • Batch 1024 ties control at half the steps (samples-fair) — neutral on quality, and the lever that may rescue muon's wall-clock.
