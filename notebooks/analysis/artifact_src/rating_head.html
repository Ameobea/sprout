The rating head: noise, schemes, and what's learnable

The rating head predicts how much a user will enjoy a show — arguably a better target than watch-probability, and it carries real weight in the served score. It is also the noisier, harder signal. This round grounds it: verify the training plumbing, census how real humans actually rate, measure the floors, and probe the first round of fixes. Companion to the information-theoretic audit.

1 · The gradient-masking trick, auditedVERIFIED

The training trick under test: items a user has in their profile but did not rate feed the input channels (mean-imputed) but must contribute exactly zero rating gradient. Three independent levels, all exact:

One genuine defect surfaced en route: for high-mean users, raw scores 1 and 2 both clip to −2.5 in the normalized channel — ~59k entries (0.02%) are irrecoverably ambiguous in the input the model sees. Tiny, but it is real information the alpha-mix destroys, and it only affects the strongest-signal entries (a harsh minimum score from a generous rater).

4 · Calibration is solved; ordering is the frontier

Serving consumes the rating head within-user only: predicted ratings rerank one user's candidates against each other. A constant per-user error moves nothing the user can see; within-user mis-ordering is the whole product cost. Measured: the model's per-user bias is small (std 0.13 z), oracle debiasing doesn't improve MAE, and serve-side feasible debiasing is slightly negative — closed. What remains is ordering, and it has real headroom.

Within-user Spearman: value-add and ceiling

users with ≥8 held-out rated items · ceiling = attenuation bound from twin noise × per-user target variance

The head's entire value-add over "global item quality" is +0.10 rho (0.50 → 0.60), against a ceiling of ~0.85 at the twin-band noise level — roughly 30% of the closable ordering gap captured. Rho on trusted users, not aggregate MAE, is the honest score for any rating-head change; MAE's mix rewards predicting degenerate raters' constants.

5 · The floor is human, not garbageFLOOR STANDS

Hypothesis worth killing: maybe the 0.25–0.30 aleatoric floor was an artifact of degenerate/one-sitting raters in the twin pairs. Rerun with both users required trusted: genuine-band σε moves ±0.01–0.05 with no systematic direction. Trusted humans with near-identical taste histories still disagree this much. Content features or richer context may shave it; no amount of data cleaning will.

7 · The new-information leverCONFIRMED WIN

If target engineering is exhausted, feed the head information it doesn't have. The rating decoder reads only the 512-d bottleneck — the presence side broke the same constraint with its EASE graft. The winning channel here is item-item structure of rating residuals (how much each user rated each show above its global mean): a closed-form EASE on residuals reaches ρ 0.585 standalone — nearly the whole NN head — and correlates with the NN's errors (0.137), i.e. it knows things the network doesn't.

Four ways to add the signal — and one clear winner

20k holdout users, trusted rows · blend gain replicated on an independent seed-2 model (Δmae −0.0064, Δρ +0.0088)

The serve-side blend beats every learned graft tried — the 256-d projection in the concat graft smears a per-item signal that the blend applies in its native space, and the learned gate is too timid to move ordering. The final recipe, pred + 0.5·min(σu, 1)·EASEresid, strictly dominates the current head in every measured stratum — the σ-ramp switches the channel off for near-constant raters, where a fixed weight hurt. Ship shape: one 144MB f32 B-matrix + the item-mean vector + a ramp constant in the model server; per-user σ is already computed for input normalization. Zero retraining.

8 · Round 2: structure probes & the era lever

Second night. Three directives: encode rating order/date without breaking the blind-bag serving contract, test whether the shared presence+rating bottleneck taxes either head, and keep pulling the input-information thread. Same instrument throughout: 20k-user floors dump, trusted MAE + within-user ρ.

Split the model in two?CLOSED — MULTITASK WINS BOTH WAYS

single-loss probes, identical arch & inputs — only the other head's gradient removed

The shared bottleneck is a mutual regularizer, not a tax. Rating-only loses ~0.003 trusted MAE. Presence-only fits kept items far better (6.345 vs 6.620 — freed capacity goes to memorization) yet predicts held-out items worse. Classic Caruana-style auxiliary-task transfer, and the SVD++ lesson (implicit feedback improves explicit prediction) reproduced inside one autoencoder. Shared representation stays.

Is it the uncertainty weighting?RUNNING

multitask with fixed equal weights (loss = P + R, no learned log-vars) — exonerates or convicts the weighting scheme

If fixed weights match the learned-weighting control on both generalization metrics, the multitask benefit is multitasking itself; if they land near the single-task runs, the training-dynamics confound wins.

Temporal input channelNULL — MECHANISM, NOT SPARSITY

3rd input channel = within-user time rank; start_date (18% coverage, true watch dates) and updated_at (100% coverage)

The signal is real — per-user residual drift ~0.18 z, idiosyncratic in direction (|ρ| 0.178 vs 0.083 null on updated_at) — but the channel can't cash it: the blind bag zeroes the held-out item's own time coordinate, and eval targets are uniform-era, so era-specific corrections cancel by construction. Full-coverage updated_at lands exactly on control (0.4504/0.6047), killing the sparsity excuse. Input channel closed; the signal moved serve-side instead (next card).

Era-slope serve debiasCONFIRMED WIN — STACKS WITH THE BLEND

per-user slope of error vs time rank, fit on serve-visible entries, extrapolated to "now" · slope-only (the constant re-confirms as harmful) · λb-insensitive

The model absorbs only half of users' taste drift; the rest sits in its errors, worst exactly where serving lives. Late-era targets (the proxy for scoring candidates at the user's current taste) run +0.024 over-predicted — users grow more critical than their profile average. The closed-form fix needs only updated_at, which the MAL profile fetch already returns. At serve every candidate sits at era ≈ 1, so the correction is a per-user constant: pure calibration, no reordering. Cumulative serve-side stack, seed-2: 0.4516 → 0.4442 trusted MAE (−1.6%) at blend ρ 0.6118 — zero retraining.
